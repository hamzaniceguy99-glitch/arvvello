The programs, in detail

Four programs of 8 to 12 weeks, worked on your real team and your real conversations.

Program 01 · Thirty minutes that count

One-on-ones

A status report is not a one-on-one. We restructure them around the other person's agenda, work on questions that open things up, and on the discipline of following through on what was raised — which is what turns the meeting into something people protect rather than cancel.

  • 10 weeks
  • New managers
  • 2 hours a week
  • A team to practice with

Program 02 · Say it early, say it plainly

Feedback & hard talks

Feedback saved for a review is useless, and softened until it's unrecognizable is worse. We work on being specific and timely, on the underperformance conversation, on conflict between team members, and on staying calm when someone reacts badly. Anything approaching discipline or termination goes to HR and legal — that's a firm line.

  • 10 weeks
  • Managing people
  • 2 hours a week
  • Situations to work on

Program 03 · Stop doing the work

Delegation & priorities

New managers do the work themselves because it's faster this week and disastrous by next quarter. We work on delegating whole problems with context and a check-in point, on saying no upward, and on a calendar that leaves room for the management part of the job.

  • 8 weeks
  • Managing people
  • 2 hours a week
  • Your calendar

Program 04 · Reviews without dread

Performance & growth

Reviews are painful when expectations were never explicit. We set clear ones early, keep a running record so review writing takes an hour rather than a weekend, and work on development conversations — including for people who aren't aiming at promotion, which is most of them.

  • 8 weeks
  • Some experience
  • 2 hours a week
